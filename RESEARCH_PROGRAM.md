## 1. Research programme at a glance

The programme develops theoretical, methodological, and cross-disciplinary work on factor hierarchies, structural-regime switching, statistical representations, information geometry, and scientific testability. The categories below describe the author's framing of the work; they are not claims of institutional adoption or scientific consensus.

### Three names for related presentations of the programme

- **Factor Hierarchy System** — the natural-science / systems framing.
- **Information Isomorphism Epistemology** — the philosophical and epistemological framing.
- **Scientific Discovery Operating System** — the methodology and engineering framing.

These are related conceptual presentations of the author's programme, rather than three institutional entities.

### Four proposed core components

| Layer | Component | Intended role |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Epistemological foundation | **Information Isomorphism Law** | Studies conditions under which statistical structure carries information about regime structure. |
| Ontology | **Factor Hierarchy Law** | Distinguishes regime factors and execution factors and studies their roles and interaction. |
| Epistemological standard | **Testability Principle** | Proposes an operational threshold for evaluating claims about factor importance. |
| Methodology | **Testability Norms** | Organizes testing across six functional dimensions while treating tools as replaceable implementations. |

The **Multi-Regime State Decoupling (MRSD) Algorithm** belongs to the implementation layer; it is not counted as a fifth conceptual component.

## 6. Testability Norms: six functional dimensions

The framework presents four core dimensions and two completeness dimensions:

1. **Search completeness:** systematically traverse candidate factors rather than preselecting a narrow subset.
2. **Modulation relationship:** test whether proposed regime factors modulate the coefficients/exposures of execution factors.
3. **Structural break:** test whether relationships differ across regimes, using independent tools as appropriate.
4. **Causal direction:** examine the direction and timing of proposed relationships and state what the selected test can establish.
5. **Problem completeness:** audit coverage of the research question (the programme associates this with 5W2H).
6. **Process closure:** document a Plan–Do–Check–Act loop, including negative controls where appropriate.

The exact implementation tools and the assumptions needed for each test should be described in the corresponding paper, rather than inferred from the list alone.
