🧭 Core Components

Four components in a proposed four-layer progression: epistemological foundation → ontology → epistemological standard → methodology. The framework is developed across mathematical papers and cross-disciplinary case studies authored within this research programme.
Layer 1 · Epistemological Foundation

📡 The Information Isomorphism Law

The framework’s proposed account of scientific knowability The proposed law states that, in objective-data systems exhibiting regime switching, statistical structures can carry information about regime structures. The paper programme treats this as both an ontological claim about structural information and an epistemological claim about how such information may be recovered. Its validity is conditional on the assumptions and model classes developed in the corresponding papers. Three progressive ontological levels: Existence (regime switching leaves quantifiable traces) → Correspondence (traces systematically correspond to regime attributes) → Precision (encoding is exact on truth-known systems). Key anchor: 2D Ising exact solution — Chow F achieves global unique maximum at exact Tc (F = 118,074); bias strictly zero in analytical sense.
Layer 2 · Ontology

📐 The Factor Hierarchy Law

What the world is — regime factors vs execution factors The proposed law classifies factors in the studied multi-factor systems into two roles: regime factors (which determine which set of rules applies) and execution factors (which operate under those rules). It further hypothesizes that crossing a regime boundary can systematically reset execution-factor exposures. The papers specify the model classes and conditions under which these claims are tested.
  • Axiom 1 (Factor Bifurcation): Factors naturally divide into regime factors and execution factors.
  • Axiom 2 (Regime Switch): When a regime factor crosses its critical value, the factor weights of the entire system are systematically reset.
  • Axiom 3 (Interaction Effect): The function of a regime factor is not in its direct explanatory power, but in its modulation of execution factors' exposure coefficients.
    • Selected case studies reported in the author’s papers: finance (China–U.S. spread), astronomy (proxy regime factor), particle physics (color charge), chemistry (period/valence electrons), nuclear physics (N and Z), and statistical physics (temperature). The evidence types and model assumptions differ across areas.
    Layer 3 · Epistemological Standard

    ⚖️ The Testability Principle

    What counts as reliable — beyond falsifiability The Testability Principle proposes a second methodological threshold alongside Popperian falsifiability: a claim about factor importance should be evaluated through a complete set of regime-dependence tests before being treated as reliable. This is the author’s proposed methodological framework, not a standard already adopted across the sciences.

    🔬 The Testability Norms

Six dimensions: Search Completeness · Modulation Relationship · Structural Break · Causal Direction · Problem Completeness · Process Closure. See detailed section below.

    🧭 Structural–Regime Foundations, Interfaces & Unified Metatheory

    A connected eight-paper reading path (2026ay–2026bf). Paper 2026bf explicitly synthesizes the seven preceding works, 2026ay–2026be. The label used here is an editorial navigation aid based on the papers’ shared objects and dependencies, not a claim that all eight records were originally deposited under a formally named series.

    Why not “First-Principles Series”? The papers do develop foundational results, but their contributions are heterogeneous: geometric identifiability, information and mechanism quotients, dynamical transitions, observability, local statistical geometry, stochastic visibility, relative module bases, and metatheoretical certification. “Foundations, Interfaces & Unified Metatheory” describes this sequence more precisely without implying that every result is deduced from one universal axiom system.
